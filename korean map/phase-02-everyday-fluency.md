# PHASE 2 — EVERYDAY FLUENCY

**Korean Roadmap · Levels 4–6 of 18**

> **Phase goal.** Move from *can form sentences* to *can live in the language*. By the end of this phase you handle housing, banking, hospitals, bureaucracy, phone calls and workplace Korean without preparation; you understand natural-speed everyday speech at 90%; and you read a news brief and write a 300-word diary with correct spacing and register.
>
> **The shift.** Phase 1 taught you to construct. Phase 2 teaches you to *operate*. The grammar stops being new and starts being recombined at speed, in domains where a wrong word has a real consequence — a deposit, a prescription, a deadline.

**Why this phase is harder than it looks.** Phase 1 is bounded — there is a finite set of endings to learn. Phase 2 is unbounded — the vocabulary is domain-specific and the listening is real. Budget more time for it than the grammar suggests, and protect the listening slot above everything else.

## 4.1 — Housing: finding, renting, and understanding the contract

**Objective.** Navigate the Korean rental system, which has vocabulary and structures that exist nowhere else.

### Module 4.1.1 — The system before the words

| Term | Meaning | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **전세** | a large refundable deposit, no monthly rent | can be 60–80% of the property value |
| **월세** | monthly rent with a smaller deposit | the normal choice for newcomers |
| **보증금** | the deposit | refundable at the end, minus damage |
| **관리비** | maintenance fee | usually NOT included in the quoted rent |
| **계약금** | the down payment that holds the property | typically 10% |
| **중개수수료** | the broker's fee | legally capped in Korea |
| **원룸 / 투룸** | studio / one-bedroom | |
| **오피스텔 / 빌라 / 아파트** | officetel / low-rise / apartment block | different deposit conventions |
| **하숙집 / 고시원** | boarding house / tiny single room | short-term options |
| **부동산** | real-estate agency | where you actually go |

- [ ] Understand that the quoted price is usually `보증금/월세` — e.g. **1000/50** means a 10-million-won deposit and 500,000 won monthly.
- [ ] Know that 관리비 is quoted separately and can add 50,000–150,000 won.
- [ ] Know the difference between a contract you can leave early (with notice) and one you cannot.

### Module 4.1.2 — The vocabulary set

- [ ] 방 room · 집 house · 평 pyeong (3.3 m²) · 면적 area · 층 floor · 지하 basement · 엘리베이터 lift · 주차 parking · 난방 heating · 에어컨 air conditioning · 가스 gas · 수도 water · 인터넷 internet · 냉장고 fridge · 세탁기 washing machine · 가구 furniture · 창문 window · 채광 daylight · 소음 noise · 역세권 near a station · 편의시설 amenities · 반려동물 pet · 금연 no smoking

### Module 4.1.3 — The interview at the property

- [ ] `방을 보러 왔어요.` — I'm here to see the room.
- [ ] `보증금이 얼마예요?` — How much is the deposit?
- [ ] `월세는 얼마예요?` — How much is the monthly rent?
- [ ] `관리비 포함이에요?` — Is maintenance included?
- [ ] `언제부터 들어갈 수 있어요?` — When can I move in?
- [ ] `계약서를 볼 수 있을까요?` — Could I see the contract?
- [ ] `주차 돼요?` — Is there parking?
- [ ] `반려동물 키워도 돼요?` — Can I keep a pet?
- [ ] `역에서 얼마나 걸려요?` — How long from the station?
- [ ] `보증금은 언제 돌려받아요?` — When do I get the deposit back?

### Module 4.1.4 — Contract language you will meet

- [ ] 임대인 landlord · 임차인 tenant · 계약 기간 contract term · 갱신 renewal · 해지 termination · 위반 breach · 원상복구 restore to original condition · 특약사항 special conditions

> **Module completion gate (4.1).** You conduct a five-minute property enquiry: ask about deposit, rent, maintenance, move-in date, and pets, and understand the answers well enough to write down three numbers correctly.

## 4.2 — Banking and money

**Objective.** Open an account, move money, and ask about fees — the three things that go wrong silently.

### Module 4.2.1 — The vocabulary set

- [ ] 계좌 account · 통장 passbook · 입금 deposit · 출금 withdrawal · 이체 transfer · 송금 remittance · 잔액 balance · 비밀번호 PIN · 현금 cash · 수수료 fee · 환전 currency exchange · 환율 exchange rate · 체크카드 debit card · 신용카드 credit card · 자동이체 direct debit · 현금영수증 cash receipt · 공인인증서 certificate (legacy, still referenced) · ATM 현금인출기

### Module 4.2.2 — Opening an account

- [ ] `계좌를 만들고 싶어요.` — I'd like to open an account.
- [ ] `외국인등록증으로 만들 수 있어요?` — Can I open one with an alien registration card?
- [ ] `어떤 서류가 필요해요?` — What documents do I need?
- [ ] `수수료가 있어요?` — Is there a fee?
- [ ] `인터넷 뱅킹도 신청하고 싶어요.` — I'd also like online banking.

### Module 4.2.3 — Transfers, balances, and the numbers that matter

- [ ] `이체해 주세요.` — Please transfer it.
- [ ] `잔액이 얼마예요?` — What's my balance?
- [ ] `달러를 원으로 바꾸고 싶어요.` — I'd like to change dollars to won.
- [ ] `오늘 환율이 얼마예요?` — What's today's exchange rate?
- [ ] `이거 무료예요?` — Is this free?
- [ ] You must be able to hear and repeat a **six-digit balance** and an **account number** at speed. This is a listening skill, not a reading skill.

> **Module completion gate (4.2).** You complete an account-opening conversation and a currency exchange, and you correctly write down a balance heard once at normal speed.

## 4.3 — Health: symptoms, clinics, pharmacy, emergencies

**Objective.** Describe a symptom precisely, and understand an instruction you must follow.

### Module 4.3.1 — Symptom vocabulary

| Korean | English | Korean | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| 열이 나다 | to have a fever | 기침이 나다 | to cough |
| 콧물이 나다 | runny nose | 코가 막히다 | blocked nose |
| 목이 아프다 | sore throat | 머리가 아프다 | headache |
| 배가 아프다 | stomach ache | 어지럽다 | dizzy |
| 메스껍다 | nauseous | 토하다 | to vomit |
| 설사하다 | to have diarrhoea | 변비 | constipation |
| 근육통 | muscle ache | 몸살 | whole-body ache |
| 알레르기 | allergy | 부작용 | side effect |

### Module 4.3.2 — At the clinic

- [ ] `어디가 아프세요?` — Where does it hurt? (the first question, always)
- [ ] `언제부터 아프셨어요?` — Since when?
- [ ] `열이 나고 기침이 나요.` — I have a fever and a cough.
- [ ] `이틀 전부터 아팠어요.` — I've been sick since two days ago.
- [ ] `이 약을 하루에 세 번 식후에 드세요.` — Take this three times a day after meals.
- [ ] `처방전 주세요.` — Please give me the prescription.
- [ ] `보험이 돼요?` — Does insurance cover this?
- [ ] `진료비가 얼마예요?` — How much is the consultation?

### Module 4.3.3 — At the pharmacy

- [ ] `이 처방전으로 약을 주세요.` — Please fill this prescription.
- [ ] `이 약은 어떻게 먹어요?` — How do I take this?
- [ ] `식전 / 식후` — before / after meals
- [ ] `하루에 두 번` — twice a day
- [ ] `부작용이 있어요?` — Are there side effects?

### Module 4.3.4 — Emergency

- [ ] **119** is fire and ambulance.
- [ ] `도와주세요!` — Help me!
- [ ] `응급실이 어디예요?` — Where's the emergency room?
- [ ] `사람이 쓰러졌어요.` — Someone has collapsed.
- [ ] `숨을 못 쉬어요.` — I can't breathe.
- [ ] `가족에게 연락해 주세요.` — Please contact my family.

> **Module completion gate (4.3).** You describe a symptom, answer the follow-up questions, and repeat back the dosage instructions correctly — role-played with a tutor who deliberately speaks at natural speed.

- [ ] `어디서 갈아타요?` — Where do I transfer?
- [ ] `이 버스 ___에 가요?` — Does this bus go to ___?

## 4.5 — Bureaucracy: registration, documents, offices

**Objective.** Survive a 주민센터 counter. This domain is where Korean feels most foreign, because the language is formal, formulaic, and fast.

### Module 4.5.1 — The vocabulary set

- [ ] 주민센터 community centre · 외국인등록 alien registration · 외국인등록증 the card · 비자 visa · 여권 passport · 서류 documents · 신청서 application form · 증명서 certificate · 재학증명서 enrolment certificate · 재직증명서 employment certificate · 수수료 fee · 접수 receipt of application · 발급 issuance · 연장 extension · 신고 report/declaration · 세금 tax · 건강보험 national health insurance · 도장 / 서명 seal / signature · 사본 copy · 원본 original

### Module 4.5.2 — The sentences

- [ ] `외국인등록을 하러 왔어요.` — I'm here to register as a foreigner.
- [ ] `어떤 서류가 필요해요?` — What documents do I need?
- [ ] `신청서를 어디서 받아요?` — Where do I get the application form?
- [ ] `이거 어떻게 써요?` — How do I fill this in?
- [ ] `언제 나와요?` — When will it be ready?
- [ ] `며칠 걸려요?` — How many days does it take?
- [ ] `연장하려면 어떻게 해요?` — How do I extend it?
- [ ] `대리인이 와도 돼요?` — Can a representative come instead?

### Module 4.5.3 — The three habits that make bureaucracy survivable

1. **Bring the artefact, not the description.** Say `이거 되나요?` and put the document on the counter.
2. **Ask for the form to be written down.** `여기 적어 주시겠어요?` — Please write it here.
3. **Repeat the requirement back.** `여권 사본이랑 신청서, 두 개만 있으면 되는 거죠?` — So I just need a passport copy and the form?

> **Module completion gate (4.5).** You complete a registration enquiry, restate the document list correctly, and ask one clarifying question in Korean when a term is unfamiliar.

## 4.7 — Phone calls and messages

**Objective.** The hardest domain for reading-strong learners, because there is no visual context and no lip-reading.

### Module 4.7.1 — Call structure

| Stage | Korean |
|---|---|
| Answer | 여보세요 |
| Identify yourself | 저는 ___이라고 합니다 / ___인데요 |
| Ask for someone | ___ 씨 계세요? / ___ 님 부탁드립니다 |
| State purpose | ___ 때문에 전화드렸어요 |
| Hold | 잠시만 기다려 주세요 |
| Not available | 지금 자리에 안 계세요 |
| Leave a message | 메시지 남겨 드릴까요? |
| Close | 감사합니다. 안녕히 계세요 |

- [ ] `여보세요` is used only on the phone. It is not a general greeting.
- [ ] `___인데요` is the standard soft self-identification, ending in a rising tone.
- [ ] The polite phone register is 합니다체, even with people you would use 해요체 with in person.

### Module 4.7.2 — Messages and texting

- [ ] 문자 message · 카톡 KakaoTalk · 답장 reply · 읽씹 leaving a message on read · 단톡방 group chat
- [ ] Texting has its own register: 반말 between friends, with abbreviations that have no place in speech.
- [ ] Common abbreviations: ㅇㅋ (ok), ㅎㅎ (haha), ㅠㅠ (crying), ㅇㅇ (yes), ㄴㄴ (no), ㄱㄱ (go), ㅇㅈ (I agree), ㅅㄱ (辛苦了), ㅂㅂ (bye)
- [ ] `~요` softening: `내일 봐요` is friendly; `내일 봐` is intimate; `내일 뵙겠습니다` is formal.

### Module 4.7.3 — Booking, cancelling, changing

- [ ] `예약하고 싶어요.` — I'd like to book.
- [ ] `예약을 취소하고 싶어요.` — I'd like to cancel.
- [ ] `시간을 바꿀 수 있을까요?` — Could I change the time?
- [ ] `내일 오후 세 시로 해 주세요.` — Tomorrow at 3 p.m., please.
- [ ] `성함이 어떻게 되세요?` — What's your name? (the standard booking question — note the honorific)

> **Module completion gate (4.7).** You complete a phone call with no visual cues: identify yourself, state the purpose, handle "they're not available", and leave a message. Recorded, with a tutor on the other end.

## 4.8 — Shopping, returns, and complaints

**Objective.** The consumer rights conversation, which is where polite assertiveness is learned.

### Module 4.8.1 — The vocabulary set

- [ ] 교환 exchange · 환불 refund · 반품 return · 영수증 receipt · 보증서 warranty · A/S after-sales service · 고객센터 customer service · 사이즈 size · 재고 stock · 할인 discount · 세일 sale · 포인트 points · 배송 delivery · 택배 courier · 무료배송 free delivery · 품절 sold out

### Module 4.8.2 — The sentences

- [ ] `이거 교환할 수 있어요?` — Can I exchange this?
- [ ] `환불하고 싶어요.` — I'd like a refund.
- [ ] `영수증이 없어도 돼요?` — Is it okay without the receipt?
- [ ] `다른 사이즈 있어요?` — Do you have another size?
- [ ] `이거 사이즈가 안 맞아요.` — This doesn't fit.
- [ ] `배송은 며칠 걸려요?` — How many days does delivery take?
- [ ] `이거 고장 났어요.` — It's broken.
- [ ] `수리할 수 있어요?` — Can it be repaired?

### Module 4.8.3 — Polite assertion

| Too soft | Right | Too aggressive |
|---|---|---|
| 아마 안 될 것 같아요… | 환불이 가능할까요? | 환불해 주세요! |
| — | 확인해 주시겠어요? | 확인 좀 하세요 |

- [ ] The workable register: state the problem, state what you want, ask if it is possible. `이게 고장이 나서요, 교환이 가능할까요?`

> **Module completion gate (4.8).** You resolve a faulty-item complaint in a role-play where the first answer is "no", and you push back once, politely, and get a different answer.

## 4.9 — Food, restaurants, and the drinking table

**Objective.** The domain where Korean social structure is most visible, and the one you will use most.

### Module 4.9.1 — Restaurant types and the menu grammar

- [ ] 한식 Korean · 중식 Chinese · 일식 Japanese · 양식 Western · 분식 snack food · 고깃집 barbecue · 술집 bar · 포차 street tent bar · 카페 café · 패스트푸드 fast food · 배달 delivery

- [ ] The suffixes decode most menu names: **-찌개** stew · **-국** soup · **-볶음** stir-fry · **-구이** grilled · **-면** noodles · **-밥** rice dish · **-전** pancake · **-회** raw fish

### Module 4.9.2 — The restaurant script

| Stage | Korean |
|---|---|
| Enter | 몇 명이세요? → 두 명이요 |
| Order | 주문하시겠어요? → 이거 두 개 주세요 |
| Request | 반찬 더 주세요 · 물 좀 주세요 · 이거 리필돼요? |
| Call staff | 여기요! / 저기요! |
| Pay | 계산해 주세요 · 따로 계산해 주세요 · 카드로 할게요 |

- [ ] `여기요` and `저기요` are not rude — they are the normal way to call staff. Waving silently is more awkward.

### Module 4.9.3 — The drinking table

| Rule | Korean | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Pour for others, not yourself | 제가 따를게요 | an empty glass is a request |
| Receive with two hands | 두 손으로 받으세요 | one hand supporting the wrist is acceptable |
| Wait for the eldest to start | — | the first sip is theirs |
| Refuse politely | 조금만 주세요 / 저는 괜찮아요 | never a bare "no" |
| Clink | 짠! | make eye contact when you clink |

> **Module completion gate (4.9).** You order a full meal for two, make three requests, pay separately, and explain the drinking etiquette to someone else in Korean.

## 4.10 — Social life, age, and address terms

**Objective.** The relational layer. Getting this right is what makes Koreans relax around you.

### Module 4.10.1 — Age and the register decision tree

| Situation | Register | Address |
|---|---|---|
| Older by even a year | 해요체, unless invited otherwise | 형/오빠, 누나/언니 |
| Same age | 해요체 → 반말 once agreed | `우리 동갑이네요!` |
| Younger | 해요체 until close, then 반말 | 이름 + 씨 |
| Senior at work or school | 해요체 / 합니다체 | 선배님 |
| Stranger, any age | 해요체 | — |

- [ ] `말 놓으세요` / `말 놓으셔도 돼요` is the invitation to drop to 반말. Never take it yourself.
- [ ] `우리 나이가 같네요!` is a genuinely warm opener, not a strange question. Age is a normal topic.

### Module 4.10.2 — Invitations and arranging to meet

- [ ] `시간 괜찮으세요?` — Are you free?
- [ ] `다음에 같이 밥 먹어요.` — Let's eat together sometime. (often a social formula, not a firm plan)
- [ ] `언제가 좋으세요?` — When suits you?
- [ ] `어디서 만날까요?` — Where shall we meet?
- [ ] `제가 살게요.` — I'll pay. (expected to be argued with once)
- [ ] `다음에 제가 낼게요.` — I'll get it next time. (the standard counter-move)

### Module 4.10.3 — The social formulas

| Korean | Literal | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 잘 부탁드립니다 | please look after me | opening any new relationship |
| 수고하셨습니다 | you worked hard | closing a day, thanking a colleague |
| 고생하셨어요 | you suffered | warmth toward someone who struggled |
| 식사하셨어요? | have you eaten? | a greeting, not a question about food |
| 나중에 연락드릴게요 | I'll contact you later | a soft exit |

> **Module completion gate (4.10).** You meet a new person, establish the age relationship, choose the register, and make a plan for next week — all in Korean, in under three minutes.

# LEVEL 5 — LISTENING AND PRONUNCIATION MASTERY

> **Goal.** Close the gap between the Korean you can read and the Korean you can hear. By the end of this level you understand natural-speed everyday speech at 90%, catch the contractions that are never written, recognise the major dialects by ear, and produce rhythm and intonation that no longer sounds translated.

## Level dashboard

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| **Level** | 5 — Listening and Pronunciation Mastery |
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate → upper-intermediate |
| **Suggested duration** | 5–7 weeks |
| **Study commitment** | 2–3 hours per day, with at least 60 minutes of that on audio |
| **Core tools** | Recorder, headphones, a drama, a podcast, a news app, a tutor |
| **Main project** | **`ear-lab`** — a 100-clip transcription and shadowing corpus |
| **Final outcome** | 90% comprehension of everyday dialogue at native speed; 80% on news; dialect recognition at 80%; a shadowing sample that a native speaker rates as natural. |

**Prerequisites.** Level 4 complete.

> **The uncomfortable truth of this level.** A learner who reads at T4 and listens at T1 is the most common profile after a year of self-study. It happens because reading is comfortable and listening is not, so people read. This level exists to invert that ratio: **for the next five to seven weeks, audio is the majority of your study time.**

## 5.1 — The size of the gap

**Objective.** Understand precisely what is different, so the practice is targeted rather than hopeful.

### Module 5.1.1 — Three sources of the gap

| Source | Example | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Sound rules** | 괜찮아요 → [괜차나요] | you cannot match the sound to the written word |
| **Contractions** | 이것은 → 이건; 하지 않아요 → 않아요 → [아나요] | the form you learned is not the form you hear |
| **Connected speech** | word boundaries dissolve: 밥 먹었어 → [밥 머거써] | you hear one stream, not words |

### Module 5.1.2 — The measurement habit

$$\text{word accuracy} = \frac{\text{correct words}}{\text{total words}} \times 100$$

- [ ] Transcribe 60 seconds, score it, and record the number. Every session. The curve is the point.
- [ ] Classify every miss: **phoneme** (didn't hear the sound), **contraction** (didn't recognise the form), **lexical** (didn't know the word), **speed** (lost the thread).
- [ ] The classification tells you what to study. A phoneme problem is a pronunciation problem; a lexical problem is a vocabulary problem; they need different fixes.

### Module 5.1.3 — The 60/40 rule

- [ ] 60% of your listening time should be **material you understand 70–90% of** — the comprehension zone.
- [ ] 40% should be **material you understand 40–70% of** — the stretch zone.
- [ ] Material you understand 100% of is entertainment, not practice. That is fine, but it does not count.

> **Module completion gate (5.1).** You have a baseline word-accuracy number for one 60-second clip, with every miss classified in the four categories.

## 5.2 — Contractions I: pronouns, demonstratives, and everyday forms

**Objective.** Recognise the spoken forms that are never written.

### Module 5.2.1 — Pronoun contractions

| Written | Spoken | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 나는 | 난 | |
| 나를 | 날 | |
| 나의 | 내 | |
| 너는 | 넌 | |
| 너를 | 널 | |
| 너의 | 네 | often pronounced 니 to disambiguate from 내 |
| 저는 | 전 | |
| 저를 | 절 | |
| 우리 | 워 | in fast speech only |

### Module 5.2.2 — Demonstrative contractions

| Written | Spoken | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이것은 | 이건 | as for this |
| 이것이 | 이게 | this (subject) |
| 이것을 | 이걸 | this (object) |
| 그것은 | 그건 | |
| 그것이 | 그게 | |
| 그것을 | 걸 | |
| 저것은 | 저건 | |
| 그것이 뭐예요? | 그게 뭐예요? | what is that |
| 무엇 | 뭐 | what |
| 무엇이 | 뭐가 | |
| 무엇을 | 뭘 | |
| 누구가 | 누가 | who (irregular — the one everyone gets wrong) |

### Module 5.2.3 — The ones that change a whole sentence

- [ ] `그게 아니에요` — that's not it (from 그것이).
- [ ] `이걸로 할게요` — I'll take this one (from 이것으로).
- [ ] `뭘 먹을까?` — what shall we eat (from 무엇을).
- [ ] `누가 왔어?` — who came (from 누구가).
- [ ] Missing these five is enough to lose the thread of a fast exchange.

> **Module completion gate (5.2).** You transcribe a two-minute dialogue containing at least ten pronoun/demonstrative contractions at 90% word accuracy.

## 5.3 — Contractions II: endings, and the verb at speed

**Objective.** The endings collapse more than the nouns. This is the bulk of the gap.

### Module 5.3.1 — The ending contraction table

| Written | Spoken | Register |
|---|---|---|
| -고 싶어요 | -고 싶어 | casual |
| -아/어요 | -아/어 | casual (가요 → 가) |
| -았/었어요 | -았/었어 | casual |
| -(으)ㄹ 것이에요 | -(으)ㄹ 거예요 | both |
| -(으)ㄹ 것이야 | -(으)ㄹ 거야 | casual |
| -시어요 | -세요 | both |
| -시었어요 | -셨어요 | both |
| -겠어요 | -겠어 | casual |
| -ㄹ게요 | -ㄹ게 | casual |
| -는데요 | -는데 | casual |
| -거든요 | -거든 | casual |
| -잖아요 | -잖아 | casual |
| -네요 | -네 | casual |
| -군요 | -군 | casual |
| -기는 | -긴 | both (재미있기는 → 재미있긴) |
| -기는요 | -긴요 | both |
| -지 않아요 | -잖아요 | historically; now a reminder tag |
| -다고요 | -다구요 | informal |
| -냐고요 | -냐구요 | informal |
| -아/어 가지고 | -아/어 갖고 | very common in speech |
| -아/어다가 | -아/어다 | common |

### Module 5.3.2 — The ones that will ambush you

- [ ] `-잖아요` is not a question. It means *as you know* or *I told you*. `내가 말했잖아요` — I told you, didn't I.
- [ ] `-거든요` explains or defends: `지금 바쁘거든요` — because I'm busy, you see. It expects no reply.
- [ ] `-긴요` dismisses: `재미있긴요` — fun? Not really.
- [ ] `-다면서요` reports hearsay: `한국에 간다면서요?` — I heard you're going to Korea?
- [ ] `-더라고요` reports something you experienced yourself: `생각보다 비싸더라고요` — it was more expensive than I expected.

### Module 5.3.3 — Word-level fast speech

| Written | Heard |
|---|---|
| 이렇게 | [이러케] |
| 어떻게 | [어떠케] |
| 그렇게 | [그러케] |
| 같이 | [가치] |
| 싫어 | [시러] |
| 괜찮아요 | [괜차나요] |
| 뭐 해 | [뭐 해] — two syllables, not three |
| 뭐 하는 거야 | [뭐 하는 거야] with the 하는 compressed |
| 그렇구나 | [그러쿠나] |

> **Module completion gate (5.3).** You hear a casual dialogue and write the **written** form of every ending, not the sound — i.e. you hear [할거야] and write 할 것이야/할 거야, and you can say which is intended.

## 5.4 — Sound change beyond the seven

**Objective.** The rules from L0 cover the syllable boundary. These cover the word boundary and the fast-speech reductions.

### Module 5.4.1 — ㄴ insertion (사이시옷)

A ㄴ appears where there is none, between a consonant-final word and an ㅇ-initial word.

| Written | Spoken | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 나뭇잎 | [나문닙] | tree leaf |
| 꽃잎 | [꼰닙] | flower petal |
| 한여름 | [한녀름] | midsummer |
| 솜이불 | [솜니불] | cotton quilt |
| 신여성 | [신녀성] | new woman |
| 색연필 | [생년필] | coloured pencil |
| 담요 | [담뇨] | blanket |
| 식용유 | [시굥뉴] | cooking oil |

- [ ] This is the rule that makes 나뭇잎 unrecognisable if you are listening for [나무십]. Korean speakers hear [나문닙] and do not notice the ㅅ at all.

### Module 5.4.2 — ㅎ weakening

| Written | Spoken | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 좋아요 | [조아요] | good |
| 싫어요 | [시러요] | I don't like it |
| 넣어요 | [너어요] | put it in |
| 쌓아요 | [싸아요] | stack |
| 놓는 | [논는] | placing |
| 좋습니다 | [조씀니다] | is good (formal) |
| 은행 | [으냉] | bank |
| 전화 | [저놔] | telephone |
| 결혼 | [결혼] | marriage — the ㅎ survives here |

### Module 5.4.3 — ㄹ deletion before ㄴ and ㅂ

| Written | Spoken | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 아는 | [아는] | the one who knows (from 알-) |
| 사는 | [사는] | the one who lives (from 살-) |
| 만드는 | [만드는] | the one who makes |
| 먼 | [먼] | far (from 멀- + ㄴ) |
| 긴 | [긴] | long |
| 단 | [단] | sweet |

### Module 5.4.4 — The reductions nobody teaches

- [ ] `-아/어 가지고` → [갖고] → [가고] in very fast speech: `먹어 갖고` → `머거 가꼬`.
- [ ] `그러니까` → [그니까] / [그니깐].
- [ ] `아니에요` → [아녜요].
- [ ] `뭐라고요?` → [뭐라구요?] → [뭐라예?] in casual Seoul.
- [ ] `-습니다` → [-씀니다] → [-씀다] in fast formal speech.
- [ ] `-세요` → [-셈] in imperatives between close speakers: `가셈`.

> **Module completion gate (5.4).** You transcribe ten words containing ㄴ insertion and ten containing ㅎ weakening, from audio only, at 90%.

## 5.5 — Rhythm, phrasing, and intonation

**Objective.** Move from sounding correct to sounding Korean. This is the module that changes how people respond to you.

### Module 5.5.1 — Phrasing units

- [ ] Korean is spoken in **phrase groups** of roughly 3–7 syllables, not word by word.
- [ ] A phrase group is marked by a slight lengthening of the final syllable and a small pitch movement.
- [ ] `어제는 / 친구를 만나서 / 같이 밥을 먹었어요` — three groups, not nine words.

### Module 5.5.2 — The intonation contours

| Function | Contour | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | level, then fall | 갔어요. |
| Yes/no question | rise | 갔어요? |
| Wh-question | rise then fall on the last syllable | 어디 갔어요? |
| Confirmation tag | rise | 그렇죠? |
| Surprise | high rise | 정말요? |
| Listing | rise on each item, fall on the last | 사과, 배, 감 |
| Impatience | lengthened final syllable | 빨리 해요~ |
| Warmth | raised pitch across the whole phrase | 고마워요~ |

### Module 5.5.3 — What English speakers do wrong

| Habit imported from English | Korean effect | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stress on one syllable per word | sounds mechanical | even syllable weight |
| Falling contour on questions | sounds like a statement | rise on yes/no |
| Reducing unstressed vowels to schwa | words become unintelligible | keep every vowel full |
| Pausing between words | sounds like reading aloud | phrase groups |
| Rising on statements (uptalk) | sounds uncertain or childish | fall at the end |

> **Module completion gate (5.5).** You shadow a 60-second clip and a native speaker rates the rhythm and intonation as "natural" or "almost natural" — not "correct but foreign".

## 5.6 — Listening genres

**Objective.** Each genre is a different skill. A learner who only practises drama cannot understand the news, and vice versa.

### Module 5.6.1 — The genre table

| Genre | Register | Difficulty | What it trains |
|---|---|---|---|
| **안내방송** announcements | 합니다체, formulaic | low | numbers, station names, fast formulaic speech |
| **브이로그** vlogs | 해요체, self-narration | low–medium | continuous casual speech with visual support |
| **팟캐스트** podcasts | 해요체 / 합니다체 | medium | long turns, argument structure, no visuals |
| **드라마** drama | mixed, 반말 and 해요체 | medium–high | emotion, overlap, background noise, slang |
| **뉴스** news | 합니다체, dense Sino-Korean | high | numbers, formal vocabulary, speed |
| **예능** variety | 반말, overlapping, slang | high | multiple speakers, interruptions, wordplay |
| **전화** phone | 합니다체, no visual | high | degraded audio, no lip-reading |

### Module 5.6.2 — How to train each

- [ ] **Announcements:** listen once, write the station, the direction, and the transfer. 95% target.
- [ ] **Vlogs:** three-pass dictation with subtitles off, then on to check.
- [ ] **Podcasts:** transcribe a 60-second segment, then summarise the argument in three Korean sentences.
- [ ] **Drama:** watch a scene blind, then with Korean subtitles, then blind again. The third pass is the test.
- [ ] **News:** headline + one story, with the numbers written down.
- [ ] **Variety:** don't try to catch everything. Track *who is talking about what*. Comprehension of the interaction, not the sentence.
- [ ] **Phone:** audio only, from a recording. Ten-second clips, repeated.

> **Module completion gate (5.6).** You have a measured accuracy number for at least four of the seven genres, and you can say which is currently your weakest and why.

## 5.7 — Numbers, names, and addresses at speed

**Objective.** The single most common cause of practical failure — you understood the sentence and missed the number.

### Module 5.7.1 — What arrives fast

| Item | Example | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Prices | 만 오천 원 | the 만 is often swallowed |
| Phone numbers | 공일공 일이삼사… | read digit by digit, with 공 for zero |
| Addresses | 서울시 강남구 테헤란로 123 | road names are long and unfamiliar |
| Dates | 이천이십육 년 시월 이십이 일 | 시월 not 십월 |
| Times | 세 시 삼십 분 | native hour, Sino minute |
| Account numbers | one long digit string | no grouping cue |
| Dosages | 하루 세 번 식후 | must be exact |

### Module 5.7.2 — The drill

- [ ] Fifty numbers per session, heard once, written immediately, checked.
- [ ] Include ten phone numbers, ten prices, ten times, ten dates, ten account-style strings.
- [ ] Do not pause and re-listen. One hearing is the whole exercise.
- [ ] Track accuracy by category. Phone numbers are almost always the worst at first.

> **Module completion gate (5.7).** 45/50 numbers correct from a single hearing, with no category below 80%.

## 5.8 — Accents and dialects

**Objective.** Recognition, not production. Korea is not linguistically uniform, and the standard you have been learning is the Seoul standard (표준어).

### Module 5.8.1 — The major varieties

| Region | Character | Marker you can hear | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **서울 / 경기** | standard | neutral, fast, lots of English loanwords | — |
| **경상** (Busan, Daegu, Ulsan) | strong pitch movement, clipped | 와/가 for subject, -노/-나 endings, 억양이 세다 | `밥 뭇나?` (did you eat?) · `머하노?` (what are you doing?) · `와?` (why?) · `마!` (hey/okay) |
| **전라** (Gwangju, Jeolla) | softer, more melodic | -당게, -잉 endings; 시방 for 지금 | `거시기` (that thing) · `시방` (now) |
| **충청** | slow, gentle | ~유 endings | `그래유` |
| **제주** | very distinct; often unintelligible to mainlanders | archaic vocabulary | `하르방` (grandfather) · `혼저 옵서예` (welcome) |

### Module 5.8.2 — What to do about it

- [ ] **Learn to recognise, not to imitate.** A learner affecting a Busan accent badly is worse than a learner speaking standard Korean clearly.
- [ ] Expect to meet 경상 intonation in dramas, in Busan, and in a large share of variety television.
- [ ] The practical rule: understand the dialect, produce the standard, and never mock either.

> **Module completion gate (5.8).** Given five audio clips from different regions, you identify at least four, and you can name one distinguishing feature for each.

## 5.9 — Shadowing

**Objective.** The bridge from comprehension to production. Shadowing is the highest-return speaking exercise there is, and it costs nothing.

### Module 5.9.1 — The five-stage ladder

| Stage | What you do | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Read-along** | read the transcript while the audio plays | vocabulary and reading speed |
| **2 · Delayed repetition** | pause after each phrase, repeat it | short-term memory, phrasing |
| **3 · Simultaneous** | speak *with* the audio, transcript visible | rhythm and timing |
| **4 · Blind shadowing** | speak with the audio, no transcript | real-time processing |
| **5 · Parallel monologue** | same topic, same register, your own words | spontaneous production |

### Module 5.9.2 — Rules

- [ ] Shadow material you already understand. Shadowing an unknown text teaches nothing but panic.
- [ ] 60–90 second clips, repeated. Not 10-minute videos watched once.
- [ ] Record stages 3 and 4. Compare your recording to the original at matched points.
- [ ] Match **length and contour**, not just sounds. If your phrase is 1.2 seconds and theirs is 0.8, you are not shadowing, you are translating.
- [ ] Do this daily. It is the single highest-return 15 minutes in this plan.

> **Module completion gate (5.9).** You have stage 3 and stage 4 recordings for five clips, and your stage 4 recording has the same phrase boundaries as the original.

## 5.10 — Transcription at native speed

**Objective.** Turn listening into a measurable skill with a reproducible method.

### Module 5.10.1 — The four-pass method

| Pass | Action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Listen once, no pausing, no writing | a gist, one sentence, in Korean |
| 2 | Dictate phrase by phrase, pausing | a draft transcription |
| 3 | Compare to the transcript, mark every miss in red | an error map |
| 4 | Re-listen to the missed spots only, three times each | the corrections |

### Module 5.10.2 — The error taxonomy

| Category | Meaning | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Phoneme** | heard the wrong sound | pronunciation work — you cannot hear what you cannot say |
| **Contraction** | didn't recognise the reduced form | 5.2 and 5.3 drilling |
| **Lexical** | didn't know the word | vocabulary, in a sentence |
| **Boundary** | couldn't tell where a word ended | phrase-group practice |
| **Speed** | lost the thread and stopped processing | shorten the clip, not the speed |
| **Attention** | wasn't listening | check your environment |

- [ ] Tally the categories after every transcription. The dominant category for the week is your study plan for the next week.
- [ ] Do **not** slow the audio down. Slowed audio trains a skill the real world does not require. Shorten the clip instead.

> **Module completion gate (5.10).** Ten 60-second transcriptions, each scored and classified, with an accuracy trend that is flat or rising.

# LEVEL 6 — READING AND WRITING FOUNDATIONS

> **Goal.** Read a news brief and a short story with 90% comprehension; write a 300-word diary, a formal email, and a message thread — each in the correct register, with correct spacing.

## Level dashboard

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| **Level** | 6 — Reading and Writing Foundations |
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Suggested duration** | 4–6 weeks |
| **Study commitment** | 2–3 hours per day |
| **Core tools** | A news app, graded readers, a notebook, a tutor for corrections |
| **Main project** | **`text-lab`** — a reading log, a diary, an email, and an error taxonomy |
| **Final outcome** | 90% comprehension of a news brief; 300 words of your own prose with ≤ 5 errors; correct spacing and register. |

**Prerequisites.** Level 5 complete.

> **Why writing is in the same level as reading.** They are the same skill seen from two directions: both require you to hold a whole paragraph in mind and to know which register you are in. Reading without writing leaves you unable to produce the forms you recognise; writing without reading produces text that is grammatically legal and stylistically wrong.

## 6.1 — Reading strategies

**Objective.** Read for meaning at speed, with a tolerance for the unknown.

### Module 6.1.1 — The three modes

| Mode | Purpose | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| **Skimming** | get the gist in 30 seconds | read the first sentence of each paragraph, then the last paragraph |
| **Scanning** | find a fact | look for numbers, names, and Sino-Korean content words |
| **Close reading** | understand every clause | sentence by sentence, with a dictionary |

- [ ] Most learners read everything in close-reading mode, which is slow and exhausting. Practise all three deliberately.
- [ ] A news brief should be skimmed first, then scanned for the who/what/when, then close-read only the paragraph that matters.

### Module 6.1.2 — Unknown-word tolerance

- [ ] Set a threshold: at 90% known words, keep going and infer. At 70%, look up.
- [ ] Infer from three cues: the Sino-Korean morphemes, the particle pattern, and the sentence position.
- [ ] `그 회사는 신제품을 출시했다` — if you don't know 출시, the particles tell you it is a verb acting on 신제품, and 하다 tells you it is a Sino-Korean noun-verb. That is often enough.

### Module 6.1.3 — The five-second parse

Before reading a long sentence, find:

1. The **final verb** — it carries tense, register, and negation.
2. The **subject particle** (이/가/께서) or topic (은/는).
3. The **object particle** (을/를).
4. Any **modifier ending** (-는, -(으)ㄴ, -(으)ㄹ) — everything before it is a clause.
5. The **connector** (-지만, -는데, -아서) — it tells you the relationship to the next clause.

> **Module completion gate (6.1).** You skim a 300-word news brief in 30 seconds, state its topic in one Korean sentence, then locate three specific facts by scanning.

## 6.2 — Text types and their registers

**Objective.** Recognise what kind of text you are reading within two lines, because each type has its own grammar.

### Module 6.2.1 — The register map

| Text type | Style | Typical endings | Markers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **뉴스** news | 합니다체 + -다 in print | -ㅂ니다, -다고, -라고 | passive, reported speech, dense Sino-Korean |
| **신문 헤드라인** headlines | noun-final, no verb | noun + 하다 omitted | `정부, 예산 확대` — no ending at all |
| **블로그 / SNS** | 해요체 or 반말 | -아/어요, -다 | emoji, ㅋㅋ, sentence fragments |
| **공지사항** notices | 합니다체 | -ㅂ니다, -(으)시기 바랍니다 | numbered lists, ㅇㅇ 안내 |
| **이메일** email | 합니다체 | -ㅂ니다, -드립니다 | fixed opening and closing |
| **소설** fiction | -다 style, past | -았다/었다, -더라 | long modifiers, free indirect speech |
| **시** poetry | -다, or no ending | line-final nouns | inversion, archaic forms |
| **계약서 / 서류** documents | noun-heavy, no endings | -함, -임, -할 것 | 甲/乙, list structures |

### Module 6.2.2 — The headline grammar

- [ ] Headlines delete verbs: `정부, 내년 예산 10% 확대` — *government, next year's budget 10% expanded*.
- [ ] They use the comma to replace a topic marker: `서울시, 지하철 요금 인상` — *Seoul city, subway fares to rise*.
- [ ] `-할 듯`, `-키로`, `-중` are headline-only forms.

> **Module completion gate (6.2).** Given ten short texts, you identify the type and the register from the first two lines, with 9/10 correct.

## 6.3 — 띄어쓰기: the spacing rules

**Objective.** Spacing is not cosmetic. It changes meaning, and errors are the most visible marker of a non-native writer.

### Module 6.3.1 — The ten rules

| # | Rule | Correct | Wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Particles attach to the preceding word | 나는 · 학교에서 · 밥을 | 나 는 · 학교 에서 |
| 2 | Dependent nouns are separated | 할 수 있다 · 할 것 같다 · 먹을 만큼 | 할수있다 · 할것같다 |
| 3 | Auxiliary verbs are separated (attached is permitted) | 먹어 보다 / 먹어보다 | — |
| 4 | Numerals and counters are separated (attached is permitted after digits) | 사과 두 개 · 10개 | 사과두개 |
| 5 | Proper names are written as one unit | 김민수 · 서울특별시 | 김 민수 |
| 6 | Compound nouns are written as one unit | 한국어 · 회사원 · 인공지능 | 한국 어 |
| 7 | Demonstratives and nouns are separated | 이 사람 · 그 책 · 저 집 | 이사람 |
| 8 | `의` attaches to the preceding word | 나의 · 친구의 | 나 의 |
| 9 | Enumerations use a middle dot | 사과 · 배 · 감 | 사과,배,감 |
| 10 | Units of time and date attach | 2026년 · 10월 · 22일 · 오후 3시 | 2026 년 |

### Module 6.3.2 — The four that change meaning

| Spaced | Meaning | Attached | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 아는 대로 | as I know it | 아는대로 | (same, but non-standard) |
| 법 대로 | according to the law | 법대로 | (조사 — attached is correct) |
| 먹을 만큼 | as much as you'll eat | 먹을만큼 | (non-standard) |
| 한 데 모이다 | gather in one place | 한데 모이다 | *all together* — different word |

> **Module completion gate (6.3).** You correct 25 spacing errors in a supplied text at 90%+ and state the rule for each correction.

## 6.4 — The written style: -다 and its registers

**Objective.** Write prose that sounds written, not transcribed.

### Module 6.4.1 — The plain written style

| Tense | Verbs | Adjectives | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present | -ㄴ다 / -는다 | -다 | 간다 · 먹는다 · 좋다 |
| Past | -았다 / -었다 | -았다/었다 | 갔다 · 먹었다 · 좋았다 |
| Future | -겠다 / -ㄹ 것이다 | -겠다 | 가겠다 · 갈 것이다 |

- [ ] The -다 style is the **default for written prose**: diaries, reports, novels, news.
- [ ] It is not rude. It is not speech. It is the register of the written page, and confusing the two is the most common stylistic error at this level.
- [ ] Speech uses 해요체/합니다체; writing uses -다; the two are not interchangeable.

### Module 6.4.2 — The three written registers

| Register | Form | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **-다 (평서체)** | 간다, 먹었다 | diaries, reports, fiction, news |
| **합니다체** | 갑니다, 먹었습니다 | emails, notices, formal letters, presentations |
| **해요체** | 가요, 먹었어요 | messages, blogs, anything semi-casual |

> **Module completion gate (6.4).** You rewrite a spoken paragraph in -다 style, changing every verb, without altering the meaning.

## 6.5 — Punctuation, numerals, and formatting

**Objective.** The mechanics that make text look Korean rather than translated.

### Module 6.5.1 — Punctuation

| Mark | Korean | Note |
|---|---|---|
| . | 마침표 | no space before it |
| , | 쉼표 | used more sparingly than in English |
| ? | 물음표 | |
| ! | 느낌표 | rarer in formal writing |
| " " | 큰따옴표 | direct quotation |
| ' ' | 작은따옴표 | quotation within a quotation, or emphasis |
| 《 》 | 겹낫표 | book and film titles |
| 〈 〉 | 홑낫표 | article and chapter titles |
| · | 가운뎃점 | lists |

- [ ] No space before punctuation: `갔어요.` not `갔어요 .`
- [ ] Korean uses fewer commas than English. Where English needs a comma, Korean often uses a particle instead.

### Module 6.5.2 — Numerals

- [ ] Arabic numerals for prices, dates, phone numbers, and statistics: `15,000원`, `2026년 10월 22일`.
- [ ] Sino-Korean words for small numbers in prose: `세 명`, `두 시간`.
- [ ] In formal or literary text, numbers under ten are often written in words.

> **Module completion gate (6.5).** You format a 150-word text with correct punctuation, numeral style, and quotation marks, and it passes a native read without correction.

## 6.6 — Writing the diary

**Objective.** Daily output with a low activation cost. This is the single most effective writing habit in the language.

### Module 6.6.1 — The structure

| Element | Korean | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Date header | 날짜 | 2026년 10월 22일 목요일 · 맑음 |
| Opening | 오늘은 ~ | 오늘은 아침 일찍 일어났다. |
| Two or three events | -고 / -아서 / -는데 | 친구를 만나서 같이 점심을 먹었다. |
| A reflection | -것 같다 / -더라 | 생각보다 피곤했다. |
| Closing | 내일은 ~ | 내일은 일찍 자야겠다. |

### Module 6.6.2 — The rules

- [ ] Write in -다 style, not 해요체. Diaries are prose.
- [ ] Five to eight sentences is enough. Daily beats long.
- [ ] Use only grammar you have *produced* before. A diary is for consolidation, not experimentation.
- [ ] Add one sentence per entry using a pattern from the current level. This is how new grammar enters production.
- [ ] Keep every entry. The Day 1 to Day 100 comparison is the most motivating artefact you will have.

> **Module completion gate (6.6).** Ten consecutive diary entries, 5+ sentences each, in -다 style, with no more than three errors per entry after correction.

## 6.7 — Messages and emails

**Objective.** Write to a stranger, a colleague, and a friend — three different languages.

### Module 6.7.1 — The email skeleton

| Part | Korean |
|---|---|
| Subject | [문의] 계약 관련 문의드립니다 |
| Greeting | 안녕하세요, ___님. |
| Self-introduction | 저는 ___ 소속의 ___입니다. |
| Purpose | 다름이 아니라 ___ 때문에 연락드렸습니다. |
| Body | (one point per paragraph) |
| Request | 바쁘시겠지만 확인 부탁드리겠습니다. |
| Closing | 감사합니다. |
| Signature | ___ 드림 |

### Module 6.7.2 — The ten phrases that do the work

- [ ] `다름이 아니라` — the thing is (soft opener)
- [ ] `문의드리고 싶은 것이 있습니다` — I'd like to ask about something
- [ ] `혹시 가능하실까요?` — would it by any chance be possible?
- [ ] `바쁘시겠지만` — I know you're busy, but
- [ ] `확인 부탁드립니다` — please check / confirm
- [ ] `회신 부탁드립니다` — please reply
- [ ] `이해해 주시면 감사하겠습니다` — I'd be grateful if you understood
- [ ] `불편을 드려 죄송합니다` — sorry for the inconvenience
- [ ] `잘 부탁드립니다` — I look forward to working with you
- [ ] `___ 드림` — from ___ (the closing signature)

### Module 6.7.3 — The three registers of a message

| Recipient | Opener | Ending |
|---|---|---|
| Professor / senior | 안녕하세요, 교수님. | 감사합니다. ___ 드림 |
| Colleague | 안녕하세요, ___ 님. | 감사합니다. |
| Friend | (none) | ㅇㅋ / 고마워 / 내일 봐 |

> **Module completion gate (6.7).** You write a formal enquiry email, a professional message, and a casual message thread, and a native speaker finds no register error in any of the three.

## 6.8 — Reading fiction and poetry

**Objective.** The reading skill that transfers to everything: holding a long sentence open while its meaning assembles at the end.

### Module 6.8.1 — The problem fiction creates

- [ ] Sentences run to 40+ syllables, with two or three embedded clauses.
- [ ] The verb is at the end, so the meaning arrives last. You must hold the pieces in working memory.
- [ ] Free indirect speech puts a character's thoughts in the narrator's grammar, without quotation marks.

### Module 6.8.2 — The reading ladder

| Stage | Material | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 동화 (fairy tales), graded readers | sentence rhythm, familiar vocabulary |
| 2 | 만화 (comics), 웹툰 | dialogue, contractions, slang |
| 3 | 짧은 에세이 (short essays) | paragraph structure, opinion |
| 4 | 현대 단편 소설 (contemporary short stories) | long modifiers, literary tense |
| 5 | 시 (poetry) | compression, inversion, sound |

- [ ] Read the same short story twice: once for the plot with a dictionary, once for the sentences without one.
- [ ] Mark every sentence over 30 syllables. Those are the ones to parse on paper.

### Module 6.8.3 — Poetry, honestly

- [ ] Poetry at L6 is recognition, not mastery. Read it aloud for sound; the meaning comes later.
- [ ] Korean poetry uses inversion (`하늘을 나는 새` reversed as `나는 새, 하늘을`), archaic endings (-하노라, -리라), and line breaks that carry meaning.
- [ ] One poem per week, read aloud, not analysed.

> **Module completion gate (6.8).** You read a 1,000-word short story and write a 150-word summary in Korean with 90% comprehension, plus three sentences on the writing style.

## 6.9 — Vocabulary at scale: word formation

**Objective.** Stop learning words one at a time. Learn the rules that generate them.

### Module 6.9.1 — The five productive patterns

| Pattern | Rule | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Noun + 하다** | makes a verb | 공부 + 하다 → 공부하다 | to study |
| **Noun + 되다** | makes a passive | 결정 + 되다 → 결정되다 | to be decided |
| **-적** | adjectival | 경제 + 적 → 경제적 | economic |
| **-성** | abstract quality | 가능 + 성 → 가능성 | possibility |
| **-자** | agent | 생산 + 자 → 생산자 | producer |

### Module 6.9.2 — The suffix map

| Suffix | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| -인 | person | 연예인, 예술인, 외국인 |
| -가 | professional | 작가, 화가, 음악가 |
| -원 | member, staff | 회사원, 연구원, 직원 |
| -실 | room | 교실, 회의실, 사무실 |
| -장 | place of business | 시장, 공장, 영화관? (관) |
| -관 | building | 도서관, 미술관, 체육관 |
| -품 | product | 신제품, 상품, 식품 |
| -비 | cost | 교통비, 생활비, 의료비 |
| -감 | feeling | 책임감, 만족감, 소속감 |

### Module 6.9.3 — The Sino-Korean preview

- [ ] Most abstract vocabulary is Sino-Korean, built from characters you will formally study at L11.
- [ ] You can already decode a lot of it: 학교 = 學校 (study + school), 전화 = 電話 (electric + speech), 대학 = 大學 (great + study).
- [ ] Learning the top 100 characters at L11 will multiply your vocabulary by roughly three. For now, notice the pattern: the same syllable carries the same meaning across dozens of words.

> **Module completion gate (6.9).** Given ten noun roots, you generate the 하다 verb, the 되다 passive, and the -적 adjective where possible, and explain what each suffix does.

## 6.10 — Editing your own writing

**Objective.** The skill that separates people who improve from people who plateau.

### Module 6.10.1 — The error taxonomy

| Category | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Particle** | 저는 학교를 갔어요 | school takes 에 for destination |
| **Ending / tense** | 어제 갔어요 → 어제 가요 | tense must match the time word |
| **Spacing** | 할수있다 → 할 수 있다 | 6.3 |
| **Register** | a diary written in 해요체 | use -다 |
| **Collocation** | 밥을 마셔요 | 밥을 먹다 |
| **Spelling** | 됬어요 → 됐어요 | see the table below |
| **Sentence length** | 60 syllables, one verb | split at the connector |

### Module 6.10.2 — The ten spelling errors everyone makes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 안 vs 않 | 안 했어요 / 하지 않았어요 | 안 is an adverb; 않 is a verb stem |
| 되 vs 돼 | 됐어요 (= 되었어요) | 돼 is a contraction of 되어 |
| 왠지 vs 웬 | 왠지 (why) / 웬일 (what kind of) | different words |
| 로서 vs 로써 | 학생으로서 (as) / 연필로써 (by means of) | status vs instrument |
| -데 vs -대 | 갔더라 (I saw) / 갔대 (they say) | observation vs hearsay |
| 어떻게 vs 어떡해 | 어떻게 (how) / 어떡해 (what do I do) | |
| 오랜만 | 오랜만에 | 오랫만 is wrong |
| 할게 vs 할께 | 할게요 | no tensing in spelling |
| 뵈요 vs 봬요 | 내일 봬요 (= 뵈어요) | |
| 몇 vs 몇 | 몇 시 (what time) | |

### Module 6.10.3 — The three-pass edit

| Pass | Look for | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Structural | does each paragraph have one idea? | reorder, split |
| 2 · Grammatical | particles, endings, tense | check each verb |
| 3 · Mechanical | spacing, spelling, punctuation | read aloud; errors surface |

- [ ] Read the text aloud. Spacing errors and awkward endings are audible even when invisible.
- [ ] Keep an error count per category. The category that repeats is your next study unit.

> **Module completion gate (6.10).** You edit your own 300-word text down to ≤ 5 errors across all categories, with every remaining error explained.

## 6.11 — Level 6 Capstone: `text-lab`

# PHASE 2 CAPSTONE — THE ORDINARY DAY

> **The brief.** Live one full day in Korean, and document it. Not a day of study — a day of use. From waking to sleeping, every Korean interaction is either real or simulated, and every one is logged.

## A. Functional requirements

1. **Morning (07:00–09:00).** A two-minute voice note in Korean describing your plan for the day, in future tense.
2. **A news brief (09:00).** Read one article, skim then close-read, and write a 100-word summary in -다 style.
3. **A phone call (11:00).** A real or simulated call of 3+ minutes — booking, enquiring, or rescheduling.
4. **A listening block (14:00).** A 10-minute audio clip, transcribed with an error map and a genre label.
5. **A conversation (17:00).** Fifteen minutes with a tutor or partner, on a topic neither of you prepared.
6. **A diary entry (21:00).** 150 words, -다 style, covering the day.
7. **An evening reflection (22:00).** A five-minute voice note in Korean: what was hard, what you avoided, and what you will practise tomorrow.
