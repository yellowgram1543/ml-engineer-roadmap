# PHASE 5 — SPECIALIZATION

**Korean Roadmap · Levels 13–14 of 18**

> **Phase goal.** Up to now, every level has been about the language itself. This phase splits it by *purpose*. By the end you can run a meeting, present to a client, negotiate, write a business proposal, and interview in Korean — and you can also read a novel chapter, a journal abstract, and a political editorial with full comprehension.
>
> **The shift.** You are no longer learning Korean. You are learning *two Koreans*: the institutional one and the literary one. They share grammar and diverge almost completely in vocabulary, rhythm, and what counts as a good sentence. A manager's Korean and a novelist's Korean are not the same language spoken at different speeds.
>
> **Why both, even if you only need one.** The professional register is what you use to earn. The literary register is what makes the professional one legible — because newspapers, editorials, and any marketing copy that is any good are written in the literary voice.

# LEVEL 13 — PROFESSIONAL KOREAN

> **Goal.** Operate inside a Korean organisation. Not "survive a business trip" — function as a colleague: attend and run meetings, present to a room that outranks you, write documents that get approved, negotiate without rupturing the relationship, and interview for a job.

## 13.1 — The organisation as a system

**Objective.** Understand the structure before the vocabulary, because every sentence you say at work is shaped by it.

### Module 13.1.1 — 직급: the rank ladder

| Rank | Korean | Typical English gloss | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 사원 | 사원 | staff, entry level | the start |
| 대리 | 대리 | assistant manager | ~3–6 years |
| 과장 | 과장 | manager | ~7–10 years |
| 차장 | 차장 | deputy general manager | ~10–15 years |
| 부장 | 부장 | general manager | ~15–20 years |
| 이사 | 이사 | director | executive |
| 상무 | 상무 | executive director | |
| 전무 | 전무 | senior executive director | |
| 부사장 | 부사장 | vice president | |
| 사장 | 사장 | president | |
| 회장 | 회장 | chairman | |

- [ ] Rank determines register, seating, who speaks first, who pours, and who pays. It is not decoration.
- [ ] A person's rank is part of their name in address: `김 부장님`, never `김 씨` to a superior.
- [ ] Modern companies (startups, foreign firms) flatten this. Ask which culture you are in; do not assume.

### Module 13.1.2 — 호칭: how to address people

| Situation | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Superior with a title | surname + title + 님 | 김 과장님 |
| Superior without a title | 이름 + 님 (rare) or 선배님 | |
| Peer, same rank | 이름 + 씨, or 이름 + 님 | 지민 씨 |
| Junior colleague | 이름 + 씨, or just 이름 once close | |
| Client | 회사명 + 직함 + 님 | 삼성 김 부장님 |
| Unknown | 선생님 | |

- [ ] **Never** use a person's bare name at work unless they are clearly junior and you are close.
- [ ] `님` attaches to titles, not to surnames alone. `김 님` is wrong; `김 부장님` is right.
- [ ] When in doubt: `선생님` is never wrong and never too formal.

### Module 13.1.3 — The unwritten rules

| Rule | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| The senior speaks last in a decision | do not conclude before they have |
| The junior summarises | the youngest often takes notes and reports |
| Disagreement is indirect | "그 부분은 조금 더 검토해 볼 필요가 있을 것 같습니다" |
| Refusal is a delay | "검토해 보겠습니다" usually means no |
| The team is the unit | "제가" is used less than "저희가" |
| Face is protected publicly | correction happens privately, never in a meeting |

- [ ] `저희` (our, humble) is the default for company, team, and department: `저희 팀`, `저희 회사`.
- [ ] `우리` is warmer and used inside a team, but `저희` is safer with clients.

> **Module completion gate (13.1).** You explain the rank ladder, address ten people correctly by rank and relationship, and identify four indirect refusals in a meeting transcript.

## 13.2 — Meetings

**Objective.** Participate, then run one.

### Module 13.2.1 — The vocabulary

- [ ] 회의 meeting · 회의실 meeting room · 안건 agenda item · 발제 presentation of a topic · 논의 discussion · 결정 decision · 보고 report · 피드백 feedback · 액션 아이템 action item · 마감 deadline · 참석 attendance · 불참 absence · 대리 참석 attending on behalf · 속기 minutes · 정리 summary

### Module 13.2.2 — The meeting script

| Stage | Korean |
|---|---|
| Open | 오늘 회의를 시작하겠습니다. 안건은 세 가지입니다. |
| Introduce an item | 첫 번째 안건은 ___입니다. |
| Give the floor | ___ 님, 먼저 말씀해 주시겠어요? |
| Report | 현재 상황을 간단히 보고드리겠습니다. |
| Ask | 이 부분에 대해 어떻게 생각하십니까? |
| Disagree (soft) | 말씀은 이해했습니다만, 한 가지 우려되는 점이 있습니다. |
| Decide | 그럼 이렇게 정리하겠습니다. |
| Assign | 이 부분은 ___ 님이 맡아 주시겠습니까? |
| Close | 이상으로 회의를 마치겠습니다. 수고하셨습니다. |

### Module 13.2.3 — Contributing as the most junior person in the room

- [ ] You will be the least senior. Your job is not to be quiet; it is to be **specific and brief**.
- [ ] The two moves that work: ask a clarifying question (`확인차 여쭤보겠습니다만, ___은 어느 정도 규모인가요?`), and offer a concrete fact (`제가 확인해 보니 ___였습니다`).
- [ ] Never open a disagreement with `아니요`. Open with `말씀 감사합니다. 다만 …`
- [ ] If you have not understood an action item, say so immediately: `제가 이해한 게 맞는지 확인하고 싶습니다.`

> **Module completion gate (13.2).** You participate in a 20-minute simulated meeting: make two clarifying questions, one contribution, and one soft disagreement, and correctly summarise the action items assigned to you.

## 13.3 — Presentations

**Objective.** Fifteen minutes in front of a room that outranks you, in 합니다체, with slides.

### Module 13.3.1 — The structure

| Part | Korean | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Greeting and framing | 안녕하십니까. ___팀의 ___입니다. 오늘은 ___에 대해 말씀드리겠습니다. | 1 min |
| Agenda | 크게 세 부분으로 나누어 말씀드리겠습니다. | 30 s |
| Context | 먼저 배경부터 설명드리겠습니다. | 2 min |
| Body 1 | 첫째로, ___입니다. | 4 min |
| Body 2 | 둘째로, ___입니다. | 4 min |
| Implication | 이를 바탕으로 ___을 제안드리고자 합니다. | 2 min |
| Close | 정리하겠습니다. / 이상입니다. 감사합니다. | 1 min |
| Q&A | 질문 있으시면 말씀해 주시기 바랍니다. | open |

### Module 13.3.2 — The presentation register

| Function | Korean |
|---|---|
| Referring to a slide | 다음 슬라이드를 봐 주시기 바랍니다. |
| Emphasising | 특히 주목할 부분은 ___입니다. |
| Hedging a claim | 현재 데이터로는 ___라고 판단됩니다. |
| Admitting a limit | 다만 ___에 대해서는 추가 검토가 필요합니다. |
| Deferring a question | 좋은 질문입니다. 그 부분은 별도로 확인해서 말씀드리겠습니다. |
| Handling "I don't know" | 확인 후 다시 말씀드리겠습니다. |
| Redirecting | 조금 뒤에 관련 내용이 나옵니다. |

### Module 13.3.3 — What Korean audiences expect

| Expectation | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| The conclusion early | state the recommendation in the first two minutes |
| Humility markers | 과도한 자신감 reads badly; use -라고 판단됩니다 |
| Structure announced | say how many parts before you begin |
| Numbers with context | a number alone is not an argument |
| A clear ask | end with what you want, explicitly |

> **Module completion gate (13.3).** A 15-minute presentation on a real topic, recorded, with the structure above and eight presentation phrases used naturally.

## 13.4 — Business writing

**Objective.** Documents that get approved on the first pass.

### Module 13.4.1 — The four document types

| Type | Korean | Purpose | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **이메일** | email | request, inform, confirm | 100–200 words |
| **보고서** | report | status, analysis, recommendation | 1,000–3,000 words |
| **기획서** | proposal | what we will build and why | 1,500–3,000 words |
| **공지** | notice | one-way information | 100–300 words |

### Module 13.4.2 — The report structure

| Section | Korean | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 제목 | title | the conclusion, not the topic |
| 요약 | executive summary | three sentences: situation, finding, recommendation |
| 배경 | background | why this now |
| 현황 | current state | data, with sources |
| 분석 | analysis | what the data means |
| 문제점 | issues | what is wrong, precisely |
| 개선안 | recommendation | the proposal, with options and a recommendation |
| 기대효과 | expected effect | quantified where possible |
| 일정 | timeline | dates and owners |

- [ ] The title states the conclusion: `A/B 테스트 결과, 신규 UI가 전환율 12% 개선` — not `A/B 테스트 결과 보고`.
- [ ] The 요약 must stand alone. Many executives read only it.

### Module 13.4.3 — The email formulas

| Function | Korean |
|---|---|
| Subject | [요청] ___ 관련 자료 송부의 건 |
| Opening | 안녕하세요, ___ 님. ___팀의 ___입니다. |
| Context | 다름이 아니라, ___와 관련하여 연락드렸습니다. |
| Request | 바쁘시겠지만, ___을 부탁드리고자 합니다. |
| Deadline | ___까지 회신 주시면 감사하겠습니다. |
| Attachment | 자료를 첨부드립니다. |
| Apology | 답변이 늦어 죄송합니다. |
| Closing | 확인 부탁드립니다. 감사합니다. |
| Signature | ___ 드림 |

- [ ] `드림` is the standard sign-off. `올림` is more formal and older-fashioned.
- [ ] Subject lines carry the action and the object, in brackets. `[보고]`, `[요청]`, `[회신]`, `[긴급]`.

> **Module completion gate (13.4).** You write a 200-word request email and a 1,500-word report, both approved by a professional native reviewer with no register or structure corrections.

## 13.5 — Negotiation and persuasion

**Objective.** Get what you need without damaging the relationship, which in Korean is the only definition of a successful negotiation.

### Module 13.5.1 — The vocabulary

- [ ] 협상 negotiation · 조건 condition · 양보 concession · 조율 coordination · 합의 agreement · 절충 compromise · 대안 alternative · 요구 demand · 우선순위 priority · 마지노선 bottom line · 윈윈 win-win · 장기적 long-term

### Module 13.5.2 — The moves

| Move | Korean |
|---|---|
| State the position | 저희 입장은 ___입니다. |
| Anchor softly | 가능하다면 ___ 정도가 좋겠습니다. |
| Acknowledge theirs | 그 부분은 충분히 이해합니다. |
| Name the constraint | 다만 저희도 ___이라는 상황이라 쉽지 않습니다. |
| Propose a trade | 대신 ___을 조정할 수 있다면 가능합니다. |
| Offer a conditional | 만약 ___해 주신다면 저희도 ___하겠습니다. |
| Buy time | 내부적으로 검토한 뒤 다시 말씀드리겠습니다. |
| Close | 이 조건으로 정리해도 괜찮으시겠습니까? |

### Module 13.5.3 — The three rules

1. **Never say no directly.** Say `어렵습니다` and immediately offer an alternative.
2. **The silence after an offer is a tool.** Do not fill it.
3. **Leave them a way to agree.** Frame the concession as their decision, not your demand: `이렇게 하시면 어떨까요?`

> **Module completion gate (13.5).** A 15-minute negotiation role-play where you must obtain a 20% discount or a deadline extension, recorded, with both an alternative and a conditional offer used.

## 13.6 — Workplace messaging and the phone

**Objective.** The medium where most professional Korean actually happens.

### Module 13.6.1 — The messenger register

| Recipient | Opener | Ending | Emoji |
|---|---|---|---|
| Superior | 안녕하세요, ___ 님. | 감사합니다. | no |
| Peer | 안녕하세요, ___ 님. | 감사합니다 / 넵 | rarely |
| Junior | ___ 님, 안녕하세요. | 감사합니다 | sometimes |
| Group chat | (announcement format) | — | no |

- [ ] `넵` is the standard acknowledgement — it is polite, not slang. `네` is slightly formal, `응` is 반말.
- [ ] `확인했습니다` and `알겠습니다` are the two acknowledgements that matter. `알겠습니다` accepts the task; `확인했습니다` only confirms receipt.
- [ ] Voice notes are normal in Korean workplace messaging. Record one short and clear, never rambling.

### Module 13.6.2 — The work phone call

| Stage | Korean |
|---|---|
| Answer | ___입니다. (company name, not 여보세요) |
| Identify caller's need | 무엇을 도와드릴까요? |
| Transfer | 담당자에게 연결해 드리겠습니다. |
| Hold | 잠시만 기다려 주시겠습니까? |
| Not available | 지금 자리에 안 계신데, 메모 남겨 드릴까요? |
| Take a message | 성함과 연락처를 말씀해 주시겠어요? |
| Repeat back | 확인차 다시 말씀드리면, ___이 맞으시죠? |
| Close | 감사합니다. 안녕히 계세요. |

> **Module completion gate (13.6).** You take a message on a work call, repeat it back accurately, and send the corresponding chat message in the correct register.

## 13.7 — Workplace social life

**Objective.** The 20% of work that determines whether people trust you.

### Module 13.7.1 — 회식 dynamics

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Seating | senior sits farthest from the door |
| Pouring | you pour for others; never for yourself |
| Receiving | two hands, or one hand supporting the glass |
| First glass | wait for the eldest |
| Refusing alcohol | 조금만 주세요 / 저는 물로 하겠습니다 — never a bare no |
| Second round | declining is normal; leaving early needs a reason and a bow |

### Module 13.7.2 — Small talk that works

- [ ] **Safe topics:** weather, food, weekend plans, sports, travel, the commute.
- [ ] **Ask about them, not yourself:** `주말에 뭐 하셨어요?` is the workhorse.
- [ ] **Avoid:** politics, religion, salary, someone's age unless they raise it, and personal appearance.
- [ ] **Self-deprecation works.** Korean humility norms mean a small joke at your own expense lands well: `제 한국어는 아직 멀었어요.`

> **Module completion gate (13.7).** You sustain fifteen minutes of workplace small talk with a native, covering four safe topics, with no dead air over four seconds.

## 13.8 — Getting the job

**Objective.** The full Korean hiring pipeline, from 이력서 to 면접 to 연봉 협상.

### Module 13.8.1 — The documents

| Document | Korean | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Résumé | 이력서 | one page, photo, education-first for new grads |
| Cover letter | 자기소개서 | the most important document in Korean hiring |
| Portfolio | 포트폴리오 | standard for technical roles |
| Career description | 경력기술서 | for experienced hires |

**The 자기소개서 structure** — this is a genre with expected sections:

| Section | Korean | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | 성장과정 | your background, briefly, in a way that explains your values |
| Motivation | 지원동기 | why this company, specifically |
| Strengths and weaknesses | 성격의 장단점 | a real weakness with a fix |
| Experience | 경력사항 / 직무 역량 | what you did and what it produced |
| Aspiration | 입사 후 포부 | what you will do in the first two years |

- [ ] Korean cover letters are formulaic by design. The formula is not optional; the differentiation happens inside it.
- [ ] Quantify everything. `매출 15% 증가에 기여` beats `열심히 일했습니다`.

### Module 13.8.2 — The interview

| Question | Korean | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| Self-introduction | 자기소개를 해 주세요. | structure and confidence |
| Why us | 우리 회사에 지원한 이유는? | research |
| Strengths | 본인의 강점은? | self-knowledge |
| Weaknesses | 단점은 무엇입니까? | honesty plus a fix |
| Conflict | 갈등을 해결한 경험이 있습니까? | judgement |
| Failure | 실패한 경험과 배운 점은? | learning |
| Ambition | 5년 후 목표는? | commitment |
| Reverse question | 궁금한 점 있으십니까? | preparation |

- [ ] The 90-second self-introduction: name and background → one concrete achievement with a number → why this role → close with enthusiasm. Rehearse to exactly 90 seconds.
- [ ] Always have two questions ready, and never ask about salary in the first interview.
- [ ] `궁금한 점 없습니다` is a weak answer. Have questions.

### Module 13.8.3 — Salary and negotiation

| Stage | Korean |
|---|---|
| Ask | 연봉 협상이 가능한가요? |
| Give a band | 희망 연봉은 ___ 정도입니다. |
| Explain | 현재 ___을 받고 있고, 업무 범위를 고려하면 ___입니다. |
| Deflect early | 면접을 더 진행한 뒤 논의하는 게 좋을 것 같습니다. |

> **Module completion gate (13.8).** A complete mock interview in Korean — self-introduction, six questions, two reverse questions — plus a written 자기소개서, reviewed by a native.

## 13.9 — Clients and customers

**Objective.** The register of being externally accountable — the most formal Korean you will produce.

### Module 13.9.1 — The client register

| Function | Korean |
|---|---|
| Apology | 불편을 끼쳐 드려 진심으로 사과드립니다. |
| Acknowledging | 고객님의 말씀 충분히 이해했습니다. |
| Explaining | 확인해 본 결과, ___였습니다. |
| Committing | ___까지 처리해 드리도록 하겠습니다. |
| Escalating | 담당 부서에 전달하여 다시 연락드리겠습니다. |
| Refusing | 이 부분은 규정상 어렵습니다만, 대신 ___이 가능합니다. |
| Closing | 다시 한 번 불편을 드려 죄송합니다. |

- [ ] Client-facing Korean uses `-습니다` throughout, `드리다`, and a lot of softeners. It is the most formulaic register in the language, and formulas are your friend.

## 13.10 — Cross-cultural pragmatics at work

**Objective.** The layer beneath the grammar: what your Korean colleagues are actually doing.

### Module 13.10.1 — The five patterns to read

| Pattern | Surface | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| 검토해 보겠습니다 | we'll review it | probably no |
| 좀 어려울 것 같습니다 | might be difficult | no |
| 긍정적으로 검토하겠습니다 | we'll look positively | not decided |
| 그렇게 하시죠 | let's do that | agreement, sometimes resigned |
| 한번 생각해 보겠습니다 | I'll think about it | no, or a real delay |

### Module 13.10.2 — What foreigners get wrong

| Error | Effect | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Being blunt | heard as hostile | soften with `-것 같습니다` |
| Taking "I'll review" as yes | planning around a no | ask `그럼 언제쯤 가능할까요?` |
| Correcting a senior in a meeting | public loss of face | correct privately, afterwards |
| Over-explaining your reasoning | reads as defensive | one reason, then the ask |
| Declining to drink | fine, but decline with a reason | `내일 아침 일정이 있어서요` |

> **Module completion gate (13.10).** You interpret ten indirect utterances correctly, and respond to a refusal by asking for a concrete alternative.

# LEVEL 14 — MEDIA, LITERATURE, AND ACADEMIC KOREAN

> **Goal.** Read the Korean that is written to be *read*, not spoken. By the end of this level you read a newspaper editorial and identify its argument, a short story and identify its narrative technique, a journal abstract and extract the method, and a poem and hear what it does with sound — and you can write an abstract and a literary analysis of your own.

## Level dashboard

| Item | Details |
|---|---|

| **Difficulty** | Mastery-track |
| **Suggested duration** | 8–12 weeks |
| **Study commitment** | 2–3 hours per day |

> **The three registers of written Korean.** Newspaper prose is dense and nominal. Literary prose is long and sensual. Academic prose is impersonal and hedged. They share grammar and almost nothing else — and a learner who reads only news will find a novel impenetrable, because fiction is built from the *sentence*, not the information.

## 14.1 — Newspaper and current affairs

**Objective.** Read a front page the way a Korean reads it: fast, sceptically, and with the register decoded.

### Module 14.1.1 — The anatomy of a news article

| Part | Function | Language marker |
|---|---|---|
| **제목** headline | the claim | noun-final, verb deleted |
| **부제** subheadline | the qualifier | noun phrase |
| **리드** lead | who, what, when, where, why | 합니다체 or -다 |
| **본문** body | details, reverse-pyramid order | reported speech dominates |
| **인용** quotes | attribution | -라고 밝혔다 / -다고 전했다 |
| **배경** context | why it matters | -ㄴ 것으로 보인다 |

### Module 14.1.2 — Headline grammar, decoded

| Headline | Literal | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| 政府, 明年 豫算 10% 擴大 | government, next year budget 10% expanded | the government will raise next year's budget by 10% |
| 檢, 前 長官 起訴 | prosecution, former minister indicted | the prosecution indicted the former minister |
| 與野, 法案 處理 合意 | ruling and opposition, bill handling agreed | the parties agreed to pass the bill |
| 三星, 3分期 最高 實績 | Samsung, Q3 record results | Samsung posted its best Q3 ever |

- [ ] The comma replaces the topic marker: `政府,` = 정부는.
- [ ] Verbs vanish. Reconstruct them from the noun: `擴大` = 확대한다.
- [ ] `與` (ruling party) and `野` (opposition) appear as single characters. Learn these two and half of political headlines open up.
- [ ] `... 中` = in progress; `... 豫定` = scheduled; `... 檢討` = under review.

### Module 14.1.3 — Reading sceptically

| Marker | What it signals |
|---|---|
| -다고 밝혔다 | official statement, taken at face value |
| -다고 주장했다 | the paper is distancing itself |
| -ㄴ 것으로 알려졌다 | unconfirmed, sourced elsewhere |
| -ㄴ 것으로 드러났다 | established by investigation |
| -ㄴ 것으로 보인다 | the paper's inference |
| -라는 관측이 나온다 | analysts say — often unattributed |

> **Module completion gate (14.1).** You read five articles, identify the evidential marker in each and what it implies, and reconstruct the verb in five headlines.

## 14.2 — Editorials and columns

**Objective.** The argumentative register, where modern Korean prose lives.

### Module 14.2.1 — The structure

| Part | Function | Typical opening |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | a scene or fact | 지난주 ___에서 있었던 일이다 |
| Turn | the general point | 문제는 ___라는 데 있다 |
| Thesis | the claim | 결국 ___이다 |
| Support | two or three examples | 첫째, 둘째 |
| Counter | the opposing view | 물론 ___라는 지적이 있다 |
| Close | return and demand | 이제 ___할 때다 |

### Module 14.2.2 — The editorial register

| Feature | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Nominalisation | 실패의 원인은 ___에 있다 | density |
| -다 style | -해야 한다 | written authority |
| Rhetorical question | 누가 책임질 것인가? | engagement |
| Parallelism | ___도, ___도, ___도 | rhythm |
| Ending on a demand | 이제 결단이 필요하다 | editorial purpose |

- [ ] Editorials end with an imperative addressed to someone specific: the government, the company, the reader. Identify who, and you have found the point.

> **Module completion gate (14.2).** You analyse three editorials: structure, controlling image, and who the final demand addresses.

## 14.3 — Broadcast and documentary Korean

**Objective.** The spoken register of media, which is neither conversation nor writing.

### Module 14.3.1 — The news broadcast

| Feature | Example | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 합니다체 | 오늘 오후 서울 시내에서 사고가 발생했습니다 | formal speech, not -다 |
| Attribution first | 경찰에 따르면 | reported before asserted |
| Number density | 3명, 12시 40분경 | every sentence |
| Time markers | 이 시각 | "at this hour" |
| Transitions | 이어서, 한편, 앞서 | signposting |

### Module 14.3.2 — Documentary and interview

- [ ] Narration: -다 style, slow, literary.
- [ ] Interviews: subtitled in 반말 or 해요체, often with dialect.
- [ ] The two together are the best material for register-switching practice.

> **Module completion gate (14.3).** You transcribe two minutes of a news broadcast at 90% and summarise its five facts.

## 14.4 — Modern fiction

**Objective.** Read a novel chapter without translating in your head.

### Module 14.4.1 — What fiction does differently

| Feature | News | Fiction |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence length | 20–30 syllables | 40–80 |
| Tense | present, reported | past, with free indirect |
| Information | stated | implied |
| Vocabulary | institutional | sensory, idiomatic, regional |
| Rhythm | periodic | variable, deliberate |

### Module 14.4.2 — The four reading problems

| Problem | Example | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| **Long modifiers** | 어제 내가 만났던 그 사람이 말했던 이야기 | find the noun, then work backwards |
| **Free indirect speech** | 그녀는 이제 끝났다고 생각했다 → 그녀는 이제 끝났다 | a character's thought in narration's grammar, no quotes |
| **Pronoun dropping** | he/she/it all vanish | track the last mentioned subject |
| **-더라 narration** | 그곳은 조용하더라 | a narrator recalling what they experienced |

### Module 14.4.3 — The reading ladder

| Stage | Text | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 김유정 「동백꽃」 | simple, dialogue-heavy |
| 2 | 황순원 「소나기」 | classic, lyrical |
| 3 | 이문열 「우리들의 일그러진 영웅」 | clear narrative, longer |
| 4 | 박완서 「엄마의 말뚝」 | family, memory, dense |
| 5 | 한강 『채식주의자』 | contemporary, difficult |
| 6 | 최인훈 『광장』 | modernist, requires L16 |

- [ ] One short story every two weeks. Read it twice: once for plot, once for sentences.

> **Module completion gate (14.4).** You read a 3,000-word story, write a 300-word summary in Korean, and identify three passages where narrator and character voice merge.

## 14.5 — Poetry and 시조

**Objective.** Read Korean poetry for sound, then for meaning.

### Module 14.5.1 — The forms

| Form | Structure | Era |
|---|---|---|
| **시조** | 3 lines, 4 phrases each, fixed syllable count per phrase | Joseon, still composed |
| **현대시** | free verse | 20th century onward |
| **산문시** | prose poem | modern |
| **동시** | children's verse | simple, rhythmic |

### Module 14.5.2 — The devices

| Device | Korean | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Metaphor | 은유 | 나는 별이다 |
| Simile | 직유 ~처럼 | 연꽃처럼 |
| Personification | 의인법 | 바람이 노래한다 |
| Inversion | 도치 | 나는 새, 하늘을 |
| Repetition | 반복 | 가마우지도, 가마우지도 |
| Archaic ending | 고어 | -하노라, -리라, -도다 |

### Module 14.5.3 — How to read a poem

1. Read it aloud. Twice. Sound first.
2. Mark the break in each line — the pause the poet chose.
3. Identify the one image that carries the poem.
4. Ask what tone the final line sets: certainty, resignation, joy, defiance.
5. Only then read a commentary.

| Poem | Poet | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 서시 | 윤동주 | the most memorised poem in Korea |
| 진달래꽃 | 김소월 | the classic of resignation and address |

> **Module completion gate (14.5).** Twenty poems read aloud, two memorised, and a 200-word commentary written on one.

## 14.6 — Pre-modern and classical Korean

**Objective.** Recognition-level competence in the older layer, which surfaces in historical dramas, proverbs, and literary references.

### Module 14.6.1 — The forms

| Form | Period | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **향가** | Three Kingdoms | ten survive, in borrowed characters |
| **고려가요** | Goryeo | songs, dialogue-based |
| **시조** | Joseon | the dominant form, still written |
| **가사** | Joseon | longer narrative poems |
| **설화** | all | folk tales, the source of many proverbs |

### Module 14.6.2 — The archaic markers

| Marker | Modern equivalent | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| -하노라 | -한다 | hymnic, self-assured |
| -리라 | -겠다 | assured future |
| -도다 | -구나 | exclamation |

## 14.7 — Academic Korean

**Objective.** Read a paper, then write an abstract.

### Module 14.7.1 — The paper structure

| Section | Korean | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 제목 | title | topic + method + finding |
| 초록 | abstract | 200 words, everything |
| 서론 | introduction | gap, question, contribution |
| 이론적 배경 | literature review | what is known |
| 연구 방법 | methods | what you did |
| 연구 결과 | results | what you found |
| 논의 | discussion | what it means |
| 결론 | conclusion | what follows |
| 참고문헌 | references | — |

### Module 14.7.2 — The academic register

| Function | Korean |
|---|---|
| Stating a gap | 기존 연구는 ___을 밝히지 못했다. |
| Hedging a claim | ___ 것으로 나타났다 / ___을 시사한다 |
| Reporting findings | 분석 결과, ___이 확인되었다. |
| Describing method | ___을 대상으로 설문 조사를 실시하였다. |
| Concluding | 본 연구는 ___을 제안한다. |
| Limitations | 다만, 표본의 한계로 인해 일반화에는 주의가 필요하다. |

- [ ] Academic Korean is **impersonal and passive**: `분석되었다`, not `내가 분석했다`. Remove the author.
- [ ] `-다고 본다` and `-을 시사한다` are how you claim without overclaiming. Master these two and your writing sounds professional.

### Module 14.7.3 — Writing an abstract

| Move | Korean | Words |
|---|---|---|
| Background | 최근 ___이 증가하고 있다. | 30 |
| Gap | 그러나 ___에 대한 연구는 부족하다. | 25 |
| Purpose | 본 연구는 ___을 규명하고자 한다. | 20 |
| Method | ___을 대상으로 ___을 분석하였다. | 30 |
| Results | 분석 결과, ___이 나타났다. | 40 |
| Implication | 본 연구는 ___에 시사점을 제공한다. | 25 |

> **Module completion gate (14.7).** You read a journal abstract, extract its gap, method, and finding, then write a 200-word abstract of your own in the academic register.

## 14.9 — Literary translation

**Objective.** The exercise that proves you understand both languages.

### Module 14.9.1 — The two directions

| Direction | Difficulty | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Korean → English | register and rhythm | flattening poetry into information |
| English → Korean | particle choice and register | translationese |

### Module 14.9.2 — The six decisions every translation makes

1. **Register.** 해요체, -다, or 합니다체? It changes the character.
2. **Rhythm.** Korean sentences are longer. Split them or keep them?
3. **Idioms.** Translate the meaning, or the image?
4. **Cultural terms.** Explain, transliterate, or substitute?
5. **Honorifics.** English has no system; how is hierarchy preserved?
6. **What to lose.** Something always goes.

> **Module completion gate (14.9).** You translate a 500-word Korean excerpt into English and a 500-word English excerpt into Korean, each with a 200-word translator's note covering the six decisions.

# PHASE 5 CAPSTONE — THE DOSSIER

> **The brief.** Produce one professional deliverable and one literary deliverable, both in Korean, both at a standard a Korean professional or an editor would accept — then defend them in conversation.

## A. Functional requirements

2. **The literary deliverable.** A 1,200-word critical essay on a Korean short story, with quoted evidence.
3. **The translation.** 800 words of the story translated into English, with a translator's note.
4. **The defence.** A 30-minute recorded interview where a native questions both deliverables: the report's method, the essay's reading.
5. **The register comparison.** One page contrasting the professional and literary registers, with a quoted sentence from each and an analysis of the difference.
6. **The reading record.** The full L13–L14 log, with comprehension estimates.
7. **The error ledger.** 200 entries from this phase, classified by category.
